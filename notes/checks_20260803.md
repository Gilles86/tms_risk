## Check 1 — the stake split is confounded with ratio

### 1(a) Design correlations

Trial level, pooled over the 8335 IPS/vertex trials of 35 subjects. `x = log(risky/safe)`
is the psychometric axis; `stake` is `average_n = (n_safe + n_risky)/2`, exactly as
`fit_probit.get_data` defines it for `probit_average_n_full`.

| pair | Pearson r | Spearman rho |
|---|---|---|
| stake vs **x (log ratio)** | **+0.447** | +0.423 |
| stake vs ratio | +0.453 | +0.423 |
| stake vs n_risky | **+0.986** | +0.986 |
| stake vs n_safe | +0.876 | +0.911 |
| n_risky vs x | +0.566 | +0.561 |
| **n_safe vs x** | **+0.022** | +0.023 |
| n_safe vs n_risky | +0.785 | +0.831 |

Stake is almost identical to n_risky (r = 0.986), and n_risky is itself confounded with
ratio (r = 0.566). So the stake axis carries a substantial ratio component (r = 0.447),
while n_safe is orthogonal to ratio (r = 0.022).

What the per-subject median split actually splits on:

| stake bin | mean log-ratio x | mean n_safe | mean n_risky | n trials |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| low | 0.702 | 9.86 | 20.58 | 4286 |
| high | 0.830 | 22.13 | 52.64 | 4049 |

The two stake bins differ in log-ratio by **+0.128 (Cohen d = 0.378)** as well as in
n_safe by 12.3 CHF. The stake split is therefore *partly a ratio split*.

### 1(a-bis) The published interaction — REPRODUCES EXACTLY (corrected)

> **Correction.** An earlier version of this file reported that `pBayesian = 0.0153`
> "does not reproduce", giving 0.0532. That was my error, not the manuscript's: I had
> pooled the posterior over draws **and** over `risky_first` **and** over redundant grid
> levels, which is not the posterior probability of any single contrast. Redone
> properly below. The published number is correct.

The interaction is a **named coefficient** in the stored trace, so it can be read
directly instead of reconstructed. For
`chose_risky ~ x*risky_first*stimulation_condition*C(average_n_bin) + (1|subject)` with
bambi treatment coding (reference levels: `stimulation_condition = ips`,
`average_n_bin = low`, `risky_first = False` i.e. risky second):

    gamma(ips,   low)  = x                         gamma(ips,   high) = x + x:bin
    gamma(vertex,low)  = x + x:stim                gamma(vertex,high) = x + x:stim + x:bin + c

so with Δ = IPS − vertex, Δ_low − Δ_high = **c** exactly, where
c = `x:stimulation_condition:C(average_n_bin)`. A **negative** c means cTBS reduces the
psychometric slope *more* at low stake — the direction the paper claims.

| contrast | mean | 95% CrI | P(>0) | P(<0) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **interaction, risky SECOND (reference)** | **−0.5425** | [−1.0382, −0.0470] | **0.0152** | 0.9848 |
| interaction, risky FIRST | −0.3518 | [−0.8475, +0.1642] | 0.0912 | 0.9087 |
| interaction, averaged over order | −0.4472 | [−0.8026, −0.0804] | 0.0091 | 0.9909 |
| 4-way (does it differ by order?) | +0.1907 | [−0.5163, +0.9172] | 0.6927 | 0.3073 |

**P(>0) = 0.0152 for the risky-second cell is the manuscript's 0.0153.** So the reported
value is the one-sided posterior probability of the *opposite* direction, at the
reference level of `risky_first`. It reproduces to the third decimal.

Cell-level probabilities that cTBS *increased* the slope, P(Δgamma > 0):

| | low stake | high stake |
|---|---|---|
| Risky second | **0.0003** | 0.2040 |
| Risky first | 0.1110 | 0.7521 |

Two independent routes were run and agree: **route A** reads the coefficient directly;
**route B** rebuilds all four cell slopes per draw and contrasts them, matching route A
to 4.4e-16. The 4-way term gives no evidence that the interaction differs by
presentation order (P = 0.69).

### 1(a-ter) But the published model has no random slopes

`probit_average_n_full` is `... + (1|subject)` — a random **intercept** only. Every
slope, including this interaction, is a fixed effect estimated as though all 8335 trials
were independent. Whenever subjects differ in their psychometric slopes, that understates
the uncertainty on any slope contrast. For comparison, `probit_order` — the model used
for the split correlations in check 3 — carries full random slopes
`(x*risky_first*stimulation_condition|subject)`.

This matters here because a two-stage per-subject analysis of the same contrast is
nowhere near significant (below), and the most likely reason for the discrepancy is the
missing random slopes. A random-slopes refit is the decisive test.

**Done 2026-08-03 — and it confirms the diagnosis.** `analyze_probit_by_stake.py` fits
one hierarchical probit per (order × stake) cell with the full random-effects structure
`b0, b1, d0, d1` all varying by subject, i.e. each subject gets their own intercept,
slope, cTBS intercept shift and cTBS slope shift. All four converged (r̂ = 1.000, min
ESS 1602). Contrasting the two stake cells (independent posteriors, so the difference
is taken across permuted draws):

| slope interaction Δ_low − Δ_high | mean | 95% CrI | P(>0) |
|---|---|---|---|
| published joint model, `(1\|subject)` | −0.5425 | [−1.038, −0.047] | **0.0152** |
| random-slopes refit, risky second | −0.4701 | [−1.171, +0.257] | **0.099** |
| random-slopes refit, risky first | −0.2904 | [−0.996, +0.403] | 0.214 |

**The point estimate barely moves (−0.47 vs −0.54); the interval widens by ~40% and
crosses zero.** That is the signature of understated uncertainty, not of a different
effect: the published model was estimating a slope contrast as though all 8335 trials
were independent. Two sources contribute to the widening and they are not separated
here — the random slopes (the intended comparison) and the loss of partial pooling from
fitting four separate models. A single joint model carrying both the stake interaction
*and* random slopes would separate them, and is the remaining piece of work.

Note also that the same split shows **no stake dependence at all in RNP**
(interaction +0.006, P(>0) = 0.57 in both orders): the risk-attitude shift is the same
size at high and low stakes. Whatever stake dependence exists is in consistency only.

**Control: the same four cells refit with `(1|subject)` reproduce the published
result.** `analyze_probit_by_stake.py --random_effects intercept --tag ri`, 2500 tune +
2500 draws (the shorter run left one cell at ESS 326), all cells r̂ = 1.000, min ESS
1411. This is an independent PyMC reimplementation of the published structure, so it is
a real check that the discrepancy is the random effects and not something else about
the reimplementation:

| P(Δslope > 0) per cell | published bambi | this reimplementation, `(1\|subject)` |
|---|---|---|
| Risky second, low stake | 0.0003 | **0.0002** |
| Risky second, high stake | 0.2040 | 0.2107 |
| Risky first, low stake | 0.1110 | 0.1663 |
| Risky first, high stake | 0.7521 | 0.7437 |

And the interaction ladder, slope, risky second:

| | mean | 95% CrI | P(>0) |
|---|---|---|---|
| published joint model, `(1\|subject)` | −0.5425 | [−1.038, −0.047] | 0.0152 |
| this reimplementation, `(1\|subject)`, four fits | −0.5193 | [−1.037, +0.002] | 0.0259 |
| same four fits, full random slopes | −0.4701 | [−1.171, +0.257] | 0.0990 |

The first two rows agree to within what separating one joint fit into four independent
ones costs. **The step that matters is the third row: adding random slopes.** The point
estimate moves by less than 10%, the interval grows by 40%, and P(>0) goes from 0.026 to
0.099. The RNP interaction is null in every row (P(>0) ≈ 0.59 with random intercepts,
0.57 with random slopes), so *that* conclusion does not depend on the choice at all.

Figures: `notes/figures/fig3_probit_stake.pdf` (random slopes) and
`fig3_probit_stake_ri.pdf` (random intercept only). Each states its own random-effects
structure at the foot of the figure, because the panels are otherwise identical in form.

**A note on what NOT to trust.** I first checked this with unregularised per-subject ML
probit slopes. That approach is too noisy at this cell size and should be discounted:
with the order split (~30 trials/cell) and with ratio stratification (~28 trials/cell)
the fits blow up, producing per-subject slopes of +34 and "interaction" means of −35.
Only the order-pooled versions (~60 trials/cell) are well conditioned (slope range
0.67–7.35, no cell |slope| > 10), and those give:

| split (orders pooled, ~60 trials/cell) | Δ_low | Δ_high | interaction | t(33) | p | Wilcoxon p |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| stake (`average_n`) | −0.266 | −0.440 | **+0.174** | +0.47 | 0.64 | 0.99 |
| n_safe (clean axis) | −0.254 | −0.469 | **+0.215** | +0.52 | 0.60 | 0.67 |

Both are null and, if anything, point the *opposite* way to the hierarchical fit. These
are reported only as a flag that the hierarchical result is fragile — they are not the
basis for any conclusion, because a two-stage ML analysis discards the pooling the
hierarchical model provides.

## Check 2 — the Fig 5a risky-first sign reversal

Panel a is the column `cause` = perceived risky/safe EV ratio, IPS / vertex, centred at
1.0 (`plot_fig5.py` line 90, `plot_decision_space.py`). Panel c is `effect` =
P_IPS(chose risky) − P_vertex, centred at 0.

`decision_space.<label>.tsv` stores **posterior means only**, so it cannot answer (b) or
(c). `plot_decision_space.py` was extended with a `cell_draws` helper that averages over
subjects while keeping the draw axis, and now also writes
`decision_space_draws.<label>.tsv` with per-cell 95% CrIs and sign probabilities. Run on
`sciencecloud_gpu4` against `cogmodels.overnight/model-flexible2_noisefix.head_trace.netcdf`.

**Resolution caveat: `--n_draws` defaults to 80**, so every probability below is
quantised at 1/80 = 0.0125 and tail values are coarse. That is ample for the question
asked (do the intervals straddle the centre?) but the exact tail numbers should not be
quoted to three decimals.

### 2(b) Does the risky-first row cross 1.0?

**Yes, twice — it is U-shaped, not monotonic.** Along the indifference contour it crosses
1.0 at **n_safe = 12.58**, reaches a minimum of 0.9935 at n_safe = 18.2, and crosses back
above 1.0 at about n_safe = 22.5. Over the full 2-D grid the risky-first surface spans
**0.9833 to 1.0538, with 44.5% of cells below 1**. The risky-second surface spans
0.9942 to 1.0661 with only **10.2%** below 1 and does not cross along the indifference
contour.

Note on the visual reading: the dominant gradient in the risky-first panel is along
**ratio**, not n_safe (at n_safe = 7 it runs 1.0538 at ratio 1.0 down to 0.9884 at ratio
3.4). A blue-at-low-n_safe / red-at-high-n_safe reading is correct only in the upper
(high-ratio) part of the panel: at ratio 3.4 it goes 0.9884 (n_safe 7) → 1.0189
(n_safe 28). Along the indifference contour the low-n_safe end is very slightly *above*
1, not below.

### 2(c) Is the reversal credible? **No.**

| quantity | value |
|---|---|
| P(`cause` < 1) at the **lowest** n_safe (7.0), risky first | **0.212** |
| P(`cause` > 1) at the **highest** n_safe (28.0), risky first | **0.900** |
| P(`cause` < 1) at the minimum (n_safe 18.2), risky first | 0.850 |

**Every 95% CrI along the risky-first row contains 1.0.** The excursion is within the
noise around the white midpoint of the colour scale, exactly as suspected. For contrast,
the risky-second row *is* credibly above 1 at small safe payoffs: P(`cause` < 1) = 0.000
at n_safe 7.0–14.0, rising to 0.10 by n_safe 28.

### 2(d) Panel c (`effect`, ΔP(chose risky)): same answer

The risky-first row does change sign — +0.0201 at n_safe 7.0, through **−0.0097** at
n_safe 18.2, to **+0.0525** at n_safe 28.0, crossing zero at n_safe ≈ 13.2 — but the
negative excursion is not credible: P(effect < 0) = 0.725 at its deepest. The positive
value at the top end is the closest thing to credible in this row, P(effect > 0) = 0.963
at n_safe 28. The risky-second row is credibly positive throughout the low range:
+0.069 to +0.097 for n_safe 7–14 with P(effect < 0) = 0.000.

**Summary for check 2:** the sign reversal is present in the posterior means, and it is
U-shaped rather than a clean low-to-high flip, but it is **not credible** at any point
along the risky-first row. It should not be used as a cleaner statement of the order
effect.

## Check 3 — the split-correlation labels

### 3(a) Recomputed from source

Reproduced exactly as `behavior/notebooks/correlation_preference_noise.ipynb` cells 8 and
10 compute it. Source: `derivatives/cogmodels/model-probit_order_trace.netcdf`, model
`probit_order` = `chose_risky ~ x*risky_first*stimulation_condition + (x*risky_first*stimulation_condition|subject)`,
per-subject (`group=False`) parameters.

Variables: `gamma` (choice consistency, the probit slope) and
`distance_to_rnp = |rnp − 0.55|` where `rnp = exp(intercept/gamma)`. Both are taken as
the cTBS difference, **IPS − vertex**, per subject. The correlation is
corr(Δgamma, Δdistance_to_rnp), n = 35 (outliers 22 and 49 already excluded).

| condition | r | p two-sided | p one-sided (`less`) | n |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **risky FIRST** (`risky_first=True`) | **−0.594447** | 0.0001662 | 0.0000831 | 35 |
| **risky SECOND** (`risky_first=False`) | **−0.328518** | 0.0540029 | 0.0270015 | 35 |
| collapsed over order | −0.510759 | 0.0017175 | 0.0008588 | 35 |

Coding confirmed directly from the data: `risky_first=True` → mean p1 = 0.55 and
p2 = 1.00, i.e. **the first-presented option is the risky one**; `risky_first=False` →
p1 = 1.00, p2 = 0.55, i.e. the risky option came second. This matches `utils/data.py:267`.

All three coefficients reproduce `notes/v8_stats_check.md` items 9–11 to six decimals
(−0.5944470, −0.3285178, −0.5107590).

**One structural note that is easy to miss:** in the notebook, `gamma` is joined on
`(subject, stimulation_condition)` only — it is averaged over `risky_first` — while
`distance_to_rnp` is indexed by `risky_first`. So the "split by order" splits **only the
risk-attitude side** of the correlation; the consistency side is the same number in both
rows. That is a property of the published analysis, not an error introduced here.

### 3(b) Which document is stale

**`notes/reanalysis_handoff.md` §3.2 (and its source, `notes/v8_stats_check.md` §3) is
stale.** Both state that the manuscript has the two labels swapped. The manuscript text
quoted in the request — "stronger when only considering trials where the risky option was
presented first (r(33) = −0.59) as compared to trials where the risky option was
presented second (r(33) = −0.32)" — **matches the code exactly**. The swap was real in
v8 and has since been corrected in the manuscript; the audit notes were never updated.
The `r(33)` degrees of freedom are also now correct (N = 35 ⇒ df = 33), which was the
third item flagged in §3.2.

### 3(c) The following summary sentence

"The effect only occurs when safe options are presented first." Safe-first and
risky-second describe the same trials, so this sentence asserts the effect is in the
**risky-second** condition.

Against the corrected labels this is **inconsistent with the correlation it summarises**:
the correlation is stronger on risky-**first** trials (r = −0.594, p₂ = 0.00017) and
weaker on risky-**second** trials (r = −0.329, p₂ = 0.054, not significant two-sided).
The sentence names the weaker of the two conditions.

Against Fig 3 it is **consistent**: the psychophysical cTBS effect *is* in the
risky-second condition. Independently confirmed here from the raw choices — mean
ΔP(chose risky) is +0.053 on risky-second trials versus +0.006 on risky-first
(`notes/reanalysis_handoff.md` §2.4), and the model-free bin analysis puts every credible
cTBS effect in the risky-second row (check 2 above).

So the two statements are about **two different quantities** that point in opposite
directions:

- *Where the cTBS effect on choice is* → risky-second (Fig 3, and check 2).
- *Where Δconsistency and Δrisk-attitude covary across subjects* → risky-first
  (r = −0.59 vs −0.32).

The manuscript is internally consistent in its use of "safe first" vs "risky second"
phrasing — they denote the same trials — but the summary sentence, placed after the two
split correlations, reads as a summary *of those correlations*, and in that reading it
names the wrong condition. Flagged as a wording/attribution issue, not a numerical one.
