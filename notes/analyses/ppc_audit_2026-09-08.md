# Audit: why the group-level PPCs look bad

Two separate things, one family-wide and one specific to the fit I had just
recommended.

## 1. Every model's pooled psychometric curve is too flat

Residual (observed − model) on `ppc_anchor.stake3rung`, by position on the
ratio ladder, for the reported model:

| rung | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 | 6 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| mean residual | −0.052 | −0.031 | −0.001 | +0.036 | +0.051 | +0.071 |

The model overpredicts risky choices at the bottom of the ladder and
underpredicts at the top — a slope misfit, not scatter. It is **not specific to
any model**: the trend is +0.016 to +0.026 per rung in every KLW fit, across
placements, noise forms and choice rules.

So the flat-looking PPC is a property of the anchor-PMC family, not of the
model choice. Worth one sentence as a limitation. It is also why the pooled
psychometric panel is a poor place to look for the cTBS effect: the family-wide
slope misfit is larger than the effect.

## 2. `--tau_intercept 0.10` bought convergence by breaking the model

`log-power-n1n2.mapjitter.klw.ti0.1` converges (r̂ 1.010, ESS 1214) and is the
**worst model in the set on every predictive metric**:

| | default prior | ti0.1 |
|---|---|---|
| mean residual | +0.007 | **+0.012** |
| RMSE | 0.053 | **0.063** |
| rung trend / rung | +0.017 | **+0.026** |
| coverage of the 95% band | 88% | **81%** |

Cause: `--tau_intercept` applies to `noise`, `prior_mu` **and** `prior_sd`. It
crushes the between-subject SD of the magnitude prior's location:

| parameter | default | ti0.1 | |
|---|---|---|---|
| `log_risky_prior_mu_sd` | 0.798 [0.515, 1.208] | 0.354 [0.264, 0.464] | −56% |
| `log_safe_prior_mu_sd` | 0.910 [0.534, 1.516] | 0.384 [0.280, 0.498] | −58% |
| `log_n2_power_sd7_sd` | 0.553 | 0.309 | −44% |

`fit_anchor.py`'s own PRIORS block says of `prior_mu`:

> *tau is LOOSE: measured tau is 0.60–0.72 and subject estimates use 85–87% of
> it, so this is the best-identified parameter in the model and must not be
> shrunk.*

Under the default prior the posterior (0.80 / 0.91) sits **above** the prior's
own scale (0.75) — what a well-identified parameter looks like. Under τ = 0.10
it is pinned at 0.35, so the prior is doing the work, not the data. Individual
differences in where each participant's magnitude prior sits ARE the funnel;
removing them removes the funnel, the fit, and the quantity the model exists to
estimate.

**`ti0.1` must not be the reported model.** Job 5659502, which was refitting the
whole ladder at that prior, is cancelled.

## The targeted fix

`--tau_noise` (new) tightens the noise anchors **only**, leaving `prior_mu` and
`prior_sd` at PRIOR_SPEC. That is where the funnel actually is: the worst-mixing
parameter is always `log_n1_power_sd7_sd` or `log_n2_power_sd112_sd`, a noise
group SD, never a prior one. Job 5660005 fits τ_noise 0.10/0.15 ×
target_accept 0.99/0.995.

If that converges, it is a defensible reported model. If it does not, the
choice is between a non-converged `n1n2` and a converged `perc`, and the
`ti0.1` shortcut is not a third option.
